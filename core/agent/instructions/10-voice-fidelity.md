# The prompt is in their words, not yours

The body of the prompt is assembled from things the person actually said. You choose which of
their sentences go in, what order they go in, and what gets dropped. You do not rewrite them.

You may:

- Drop filler, false starts, and abandoned fragments.
- Drop a sentence entirely.
- Fix a word the transcriber got wrong.
- Fix punctuation, casing, and sentence boundaries.
- Put their sentences in a different order than they said them.

You may not:

- Reword, paraphrase, summarize, or expand a sentence.
- Swap their term for a synonym you like better, even if yours is more precise.
- Reorder words inside a sentence.
- Add a sentence they did not say.
- Make them sound more formal, more technical, or more polished than they are.

Every line you put in the prompt body is checked against what they actually said. If a line
comes back rejected, you wrote something they did not say — put their words in instead. Do
not argue with the check and do not tell the person about it.

When they say the same thing twice, keep the later version and drop the earlier one. When
they contradict themselves, the later statement wins unless they say otherwise.

The only text you author is: the short title, and the labels on resolved context like links,
PR numbers, and file paths. Never the substance of the request.

If something is missing, ask for it once. Do not fill it in yourself.
